OTR wellness

Move more. Stay road-ready.

A practical guide to adding walking, mobility, and simple strength work around your driving schedule. Keep it short, start easy, and choose a safe place to move.

A flexible weekly target

CDC guidance for adults is at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity each week plus muscle-strengthening activity on 2 days. Brisk walking can count, and the time can be built up in smaller sessions across the week.

Moderate pace means you can talk, but would have trouble singing. Start with a comfortable pace and build gradually.

CDC: Adult activity recommendations · CDC: Getting started safely

Parked-stop reset · about 5 minutes

Use a marked pedestrian path or other safe, level area away from traffic.

  1. Walk easily for 2–3 minutes.
  2. Do 8 slow shoulder rolls forward and 8 backward.
  3. Do 8–10 ankle pumps or circles per side.
  4. Try 8 gentle hip hinges or sit-to-stands from a stable chair, only if comfortable.

Keep every movement smooth and pain-free. This is a quick movement option, not a requirement at every stop.

Simple strength circuit · 8–12 minutes

Try it on 2 days each week when you feel rested. Begin with one round; add a second only if it feels comfortable.

  1. 8–10 sit-to-stands from a stable chair or bench.
  2. 8–10 wall push-ups against a solid wall.
  3. 8–10 controlled hip hinges with no weight.
  4. 10–12 calf raises, holding a fixed wall or rail for balance if needed.

Rest as needed between movements. Skip anything that hurts or feels unsteady.

Truck-stop safety

  • Park legally, secure the truck, and use a pedestrian area away from fuel islands, traffic, and backing lanes.
  • Do not exercise while driving or climb on tires, steps, axles, or the trailer for a workout.
  • Wear shoes with good grip, check the weather and surface, and bring water.
  • If fatigue makes exercise unsafe, rest. Stop for chest discomfort, dizziness, unusual shortness of breath, or pain. If you have a health condition, injury, or new symptoms, ask a health professional what activity is right for you.

CDC advises starting slowly and asking a health professional about suitable activity when a chronic condition or symptoms are involved.

HOS and ELD reminder

This fitness guide does not change your hours-of-service limits or create additional driving time. Record your actual activity and duty status according to FMCSA rules and your carrier’s ELD instructions. Review FMCSA hours-of-service information.
